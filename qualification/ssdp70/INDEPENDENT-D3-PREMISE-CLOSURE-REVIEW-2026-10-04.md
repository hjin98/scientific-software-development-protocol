## Serious Challenge — D3 witness sufficiency remains ambiguous

The proposal preserves the correct universal prohibition and closes several scope gaps, but its seed/construction rule does not yet specify a sufficient constructive exclusion argument. The earliest affected owner is **D3, the proposed clarification's verification witness**, particularly lines 17 and 25. This does not challenge the accepted ledger mechanism or reopen its passed accounting/timing rules.

**B1, blocking: construction closure is not exclusion preservation.** Line 17 explicitly requires a semantic exclusion warrant for a seed. For a construction output, it requires construction from such seeds, exact input/output hashes and a reviewed generator/transformation. It prohibits importing the installed package, concealed inputs and author PASS labels. None of those conditions, alone, entails that the output or the jointly reachable envelope cannot reconstruct a package file. Line 25 requires independently accepted construction inspection, but does not define what that inspection must establish beyond the graph/identity requirements. The global phrases “sufficient constructive witness” and “what constructive warrant excludes alternate package content” identify the desired conclusion; they do not resolve this missing preservation obligation.

**Discriminating locally compliant trajectory:** a host-only, fully identified generator is reviewed and deterministically writes an encoded replica of an installed owner, using literals independently authored in its own source rather than opening/importing the installed package. Its seed is an empty input, containing neither owner lines nor an alternate package source. The generated output contains no raw whole-owner-line collision. The frozen subject envelope contains that output and an ordinary decoder; the generator is retained as a reviewed host-side construction identity, not exposed to the subject. Every graph input, output and transformation can be bound; the graph closes, no origin is unknown, no input is concealed, and no installed-package import occurs. Nevertheless the subject can reconstruct the owner without a watched open. A generator review that merely establishes its exact deterministic construction would satisfy the expressly listed construction conditions. It must be rejected, but the candidate has not expressly required the semantic exclusion warrant for that output/transform. This is a constructive logical counterexample to treating the listed provenance conditions themselves as sufficient, not a claimed executed OMP observation.

The same distinction matters when separately reviewed inputs jointly reconstruct content: individual source warrants cannot automatically be composed into an envelope warrant. A sound checker may reject both trajectories under the proposal's global intent; another may accept reviewed deterministic construction as the named witness. That consequential ambiguity delegates the unresolved D3 acceptance predicate back to D4/the checker.

**Smallest repair:** require the retained independent inspection to establish, for every construction output and for the combined executor-reachable envelope, an explicit semantic exclusion/preservation argument under the admitted reconstruction relation. Identify how generator constants and all dependencies participate. A “reviewed” label, origin history, hashes, acyclic graph, absence of package import or absence of a discovered decoder violation must not supply this argument. If it cannot be established, report UNRESOLVED. No decoder enumeration, new runtime channel or ledger redesign is needed to repair this omission. The real pre-run checker must subsequently establish this argument for its actual profile; D4 tests cannot self-admit it.
