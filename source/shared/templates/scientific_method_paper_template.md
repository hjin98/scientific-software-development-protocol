## Problem and intended use

State the scientific/theoretical/engineering question, context of use, intended competent reader where material, and the conclusions this formulation is intended to support.

## Background and terminology

Define and briefly explain newly introduced non-common domain terminology before later scientific reasoning depends on it. State assumed prerequisites when they are not obvious from the audience/scope.

Introduce non-obvious abbreviations at first explanatory use using the conventional `full term (ABC)` form. Cite standard methods/literature where that background materially helps interpretation.

Keep this section explanatory. Do not move precise normative D1 definitions, equations, assumptions, or validity conditions out of their owning sections merely to populate background prose.

## Normative scientific / mathematical formulation

State the accepted observables/estimands/objectives, governing equations/models/definitions, units/conventions, assumptions, validity regime, and material semantic invariants. Mark this normative scope clearly so supporting explanation does not become authority accidentally.

## Assumptions, approximations, and limitations

Distinguish assumptions from derived consequences. State excluded regimes and conditions under which conclusions should not be trusted.

## Scientific uncertainty

Describe material data/parameter/model/assumption uncertainty, identifiability/model discrepancy, and robustness of conclusions where applicable.

## External adequacy / validation / proof

State the evidence route appropriate to the problem class: empirical validation, proof/reference theory, standards/qualification experiments, safety margins, or other governed context. Separate calibration from validation when material.

Where repeated/aggregated evidence matters, distinguish evidence specification, evidence realizations, observations, and the assessment of what those observations can support.

## Supporting rationale, literature, and provenance

Record the evidence and literature supporting the formulation without promoting every cited statement into project authority.

## D1 -> D2 handoff

List the minimum D1 invariants, observables, conventions, validity conditions, and uncertainty expectations that numerical concretization must preserve. Record material dependency links needed for bounded invalidation and impact closure.

## Evidence and dependency impact

When this D1 authority supersedes or materially changes prior authority, identify materially dependent D2-D4 concretizations, evidence specifications/realizations, explanatory documentation, and human re-ratification/revalidation obligations. Preserve unaffected siblings and still-valid evidence.

## Open challenges

Record only unresolved material authority challenges or concise resolved rationale needed to prevent recurrence. Do not use this section as a review transcript.
